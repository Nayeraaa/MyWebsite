Online Hate Speech in Sudan: A Systemic Design Analysis

Systemic DesignStakeholder MappingPeacetech
Overview

Sudan's counter-hate speech sector operates under a profound "Structural Asymmetry." This study uses a systemic design framework to demonstrate that grassroots counter-efforts are structurally disadvantaged because five self-reinforcing feedback loops consistently favor armed, state-level actors who have integrated hate speech and propaganda into their core military doctrine. This imbalance is not solely a function of resource disparity; three of the central feedback loops operate primarily through coordination failure, epistemic degradation, and legal immunity.

Operational and financial constraints further compound this asymmetry. Short-term, project-based funding models restrict budgets and prevent long-term research-to-practice translation, forcing organizations to continuously seek new grants rather than sustain long-term social healing. Concurrently, extreme security risks and political polarization drive deep-seated trust deficits and coordination fragility. This fragmentation isolates media houses, research groups, and grassroots peacebuilders into non-interoperable monitoring silos, creating a critical "Insight Gap" that leaves system-level campaigns undetected.

To disrupt this system of persistent impunity, the report identifies targeted leverage points and recommends five key strategic interventions designed to act as balancing loops: (1) establishing a multi-stakeholder Legal Commission for Digital Rights to draft a context-specific, prosecutable legal definition of hate speech (L1); (2) institutionalizing a secure, decentralized Coordination Secretariat to repair coordination fragility (M1); (3) implementing Forensic Digital Archiving to preserve legally admissible evidence for future accountability (E4); (4) committing to a Multi-Year Pooled Resilience Fund for core operational continuity (M4); and (5) funding Community-Led Cultural Hubs paired with an Operational Security Infrastructure package to protect vulnerable female practitioners from online gender-based violence (OGBV).
